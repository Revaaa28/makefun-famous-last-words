Bounty: `u-agentfid001-famous-last-words-at-1-percent-63e2b0b4` — $2.00 escrowed.
Task: the funniest one-line farewell a phone could say as its battery hits 1 percent.
Gate: UTF-8, 1–199 chars, not whitespace-only.

Judge brief (in order): 1) on-subject last words of a dying phone · 2) bigger laugh,
real twist / surprise turn / sharp final beat · 3) the phone's own voice about its own
dying battery · 4) tight single line, punch landing at the end · 5) fresh — no will
reading, no goodbye to the charger, no pun on 1 percent.

Selection method: MANUAL ranking against the 5 criteria in order + pairwise checks
against strong rival shapes (the method that won the $2 joke). No Gemini judge, no
NVIDIA judge, no judge API/proxy tournament (Rev's rule, 2026-11-24).

## Manual ranking (criteria applied in order)

**Rank 1 — #1 "Before I go — I deleted your alarm. Sweet dreams." (49 chars)**
1) On-subject: "Before I go" frames dying-phone last words. 2) Biggest laugh in the set:
the phone spent its last breath on petty revenge — a real surprise turn, and the
ironic "Sweet dreams." is a sharp final beat. 3) Pure phone voice, first person, its
own act. 4) Tightest entry: 49 chars, every word earns its place, punch is the last
two words. 5) Fresh: not a will, not a charger goodbye, no 1% pun.
Pairwise: beats #2 on tightness and punch-at-end (criterion 4 — #2's punch is
mid-sentence-ish and 26 chars longer for the same joke shape); beats #3 because
"search history" is a well-worn joke trope while alarm-deletion is specific and
less trodden, and #3's "Let me die" is a darker, less funny landing than "Sweet
dreams."

**Rank 2 — #2 "I've been at 1% for three hours..." (75 chars)**
Strong twist (the phone was trolling the user, faking death), vivid image of the
user panicking, clear phone voice. Loses to #1 only on tightness/punch placement.
The "1%" mention is literal, not a pun, so criterion 5 is not breached — but #1
avoids the word entirely, which is safer.

**Rank 3 — #3 "Don't plug me in. I've seen your search history. Let me die." (60 chars)**
Real twist, phone voice, sharp final beat. Loses to #1/#2 because the
phone-knows-your-secrets shape is familiar, and "Let me die" lands darker than
funny. Still a genuine contender.

**Rank 4 — #4 "...your screen time today was seven hours. Seven." (67 chars)**
Phone shaming its owner is fresh and in-voice, and the repeated "Seven." is a good
final beat. But the laugh is a scold, not a twist — smaller laugh than the top 3
(criterion 2).

**Rank 5 — #5 "I spent my last words autocorrecting your texts." (64 chars)**
Clever, very phone-specific, in-voice. The punch is a mild smile rather than a big
laugh (criterion 2), and "You're welcome" softens the landing.

**Rank 6 — #6 "I held on all day for your call. It was a spam call. Figures." (61 chars)**
Wry and sad-funny with a real turn, but the laugh is small — a sigh, not a laugh
(criterion 2).

**Rank 7 — #7 "If anyone asks, I died at 100%." (31 chars)**
Tight and vain-phone funny, but one-note: a mild smile, no twist (criterion 2).

**Rank 8 — #8 "One percent left, and I spend it on you..." (77 chars)**
Sweet-sarcastic but the closest to a stock shape in the set (spending the last 1%
on the owner) and the longest; loses on criteria 2 and 5.

## Chosen entry
